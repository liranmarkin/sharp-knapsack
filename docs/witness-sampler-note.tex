\begin{center}
{\Large\bf Sampling through the witness structure}\\[2pt]
{\small A one-page companion to Feng--Jin, ``A Faster Algorithm for
Counting Knapsack Solutions'' (SODA~2025, arXiv:2410.22267), whose
notation and numbering are used throughout.}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Where their time goes.}
The total $\OO(n^{1.5}\varepsilon^{-2})$ splits into
\emph{construction} --- the merge trees of Theorem~4.2, $\OO(n^{1.5})$
--- and \emph{queries}: $N=\OO(n/(\ell\varepsilon^2))$ samples
(Lemma~3.3), each answered by descending the trees and drawing, at every
node, a split from a distribution of support $\OO(\ell)$ by scanning it.
The claim here: the query stage can run in nearly the time it takes to
write the samples down, giving
$\OO\!\bigl(n^{1.5}+\min(n\sqrt{\ell},\,n^2/\ell)\cdot\varepsilon^{-2}\bigr)
\le \OO(n^{1.5}+n^{4/3}\varepsilon^{-2})$ ---
never worse than $\OO(n^{1.5}\varepsilon^{-2})$, and below it for every
$\varepsilon=o(1)$.

\paragraph{The identity.}
Recall the merge of \S6.2: levels $A^*_0[x]$ with
$f_0(x)\in[D^{A^*_0[x]},D^{A^*_0[x]+1})$, prefix maxima $A[x]$, residuals
$u[x]=f_0(x)D^{-A[x]}\in[1,D)$ at prefix-max-attaining positions (else
$0$); likewise $B,v$ for $g_0$; $C=$ the $(\max,+)$-convolution of $A,B$;
$w[z]=\sum_{x:\,A[x]+B[z-x]=C[z]}u[x]v[z-x]$ (Theorem~6.1); the merge
returns $h(z)=D^{C[z]}\cdot w[z]$. Each witness term satisfies
$u[x]\,v[z-x]\,D^{C[z]}=f_0(x)\,g_0(z-x)$ \emph{exactly} --- the
exponents cancel by the witness condition. Hence
\[
h(z)\;=\;\textstyle\sum\,\bigl\{\,f_0(x)g_0(y)\;:\;x+y=z,\;
A[x]+B[y]=C[z],\;u[x]v[y]>0\,\bigr\}.
\]
So the stored array is not merely a $(1\pm\delta)$-approximation of
$f\star g$: it is an \emph{exact aggregation} of the product measure
restricted to witness pairs (a measure that is within a per-prefix
$(1\pm\delta)$ of $f\star g$ by~(41)). The construction, built to
\emph{compute} the arrays, stores for free an exact factorization of the
distribution they define.

\paragraph{The sampler.}
Sample that factorization literally. (i)~At the root: a coarse value
$z\propto \hat f_{\mathrm{root}}(z)\cdot\mathbf 1[z\le t]$ --- the
capacity appears here and nowhere else. (ii)~De-rounding: a node's fine
position $s\propto h(s)$ over the preimage of its coarse value; position
rounding is a monotone floor, so the preimage is a contiguous interval
--- an $O(\log)$ prefix-sum draw, no differences of approximate
quantities anywhere. (iii)~Split: a witness pair $(x,\,y=s-x)\propto
u[x]v[y]$ over $\{A[x]+B[y]=C[s]\}$; recurse with the \emph{exact} child
values $x,y$. Every draw is exactly proportional to the stored
decomposition, so the sampled measure is the one the arrays define, and
the TV analysis of \S4--5 applies unchanged; the only new TV term is the
output re-rounding of $h$ (a pointwise $1\pm\delta$, so $\le2\delta$ per
visited merge). Children with value $0$ whose weight-$0$ stratum is only
$\varnothing$ are not descended, so a sample visits only the path-union
of its $k$ items.

\paragraph{Why the split is cheap.}
Witness pairs at $s$ organize by level: $a+b=C[s]$, and the attaining
positions of $A$-level $a$ form an interval $I^{(a)}$ ($A$ is monotone),
likewise $J^{(b)}$ --- at most $M=\OO(\text{subtree items}/\log D)$
rectangles, against array length $L$; near the roots $M\ll L$. Within a
rectangle, $u,v\in[1,D)$ have $O(\log D)$ log-range: split into factor-2
classes $X_i,Y_j$; the counts $|X_i\cap(s-Y_j)|$ for \emph{all} $s$ are
indicator convolutions, FFT-precomputed in $\OO(L\,\mathrm{polylog})$
per node. A draw picks (rectangle, class) $\propto 2^{i+j}\cdot$count,
a uniform member by rank (prefix counts from lazily built, memoized
dyadic restrictions; unconditionally $\OO(L\sqrt{N})$ amortized per
node), then accepts with probability $u[x]v[y]/2^{i+j+2}\ge\tfrac14$ ---
exact. Diagonal pairs \emph{below} $C[s]$ carry $\le\delta$ of the
diagonal mass by the multiples-of-3 separation, exactly as in~(41): one
more $O(1)$ rejection.

\paragraph{The count.}
Per split-draw $\OO(\min(M,L))$; per sample, over the pruned path-union,
$\OO(\min(n_m,\;\ell\sqrt{k}))$; over $N=\OO(n/(\ell\varepsilon^2))$
samples, $\OO(\min(n^2/\ell,\;n\sqrt{\ell})\cdot\varepsilon^{-2})
\le\OO(n^{4/3}\varepsilon^{-2})$, the maximum at $\ell=n^{2/3}$.
Construction is unchanged; the second phase (\S5.4) admits the same
treatment.

\paragraph{Status.}
The identity, the $\delta$-domination, the lazy amortization, and the
$\min$-collapse are machine-checked in Lean
(\texttt{lean/SharpKnapsack/WitnessSampler.lean}); every sampling
mechanism is validated against brute force
(\texttt{experiments/test\_witness\_sampler.py}). This is a
\emph{theorem-candidate}: what remains is the substitution pass through
Lemmas~4.6/4.7 and \S5.3--5.4 and the detailed second-phase ledger
(\texttt{docs/witness-sampler.md}).

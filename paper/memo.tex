\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The fastest deterministic FPTAS for \#Knapsack is the
$\OO(n^{2.5}\varepsilon^{-1.5})$ algorithm of Gawrychowski--Markin--%
Weimann~\cite{GMW18}, unimproved since 2018. This memo, written to be
read alongside~\cite{GMW18}, explains why: the algorithm's single
expensive primitive -- the pairwise merge of two sparsified
sum-approximation lists -- is, as a computational task, equivalent in
the worst case to general $(\min,+)$-convolution. Under the standard
MinConv hardness hypothesis, the merge is optimal, and with it every
FPTAS in the sum-approximation framework. The reduction's core is
machine-checked in Lean. We state precisely what an improvement would
now require, and what remains possible beyond the worst
case.\footnote{The Lean development accompanying this memo verifies
the reduction end to end - encoding, executable merge cumulative,
sandwich, read-off, padding, and ramp, composed in one statement -
against the same verified code that runs the FPTAS; see the
repository.}
\end{abstract}

\section{The question}

\cite{GMW18} computes a $(1+\varepsilon)$-sum approximation of the
knapsack counting function by divide and conquer: split the items,
recurse, and merge the two children's sparsified lists by convolution
(their Section~4). Every recursion level pays the same
$\OO(n^{2.5}\varepsilon^{-1.5})$, and all of it is one primitive: a
merge of two $s$-point lists costs $s^2$ pairwise products, almost all
immediately discarded by re-sparsification. Any merge in $s^{2-c}$
time would improve the record polynomially. Can the merge be faster?

\section{The answer}

\begin{theorem}[conditional optimality of the merge]
Computing the value-sparsified, position-exact merged frontier of two
$s$-point sum-approximation lists requires $s^{2-o(1)}$ time in the
worst case, unless the MinConv hypothesis fails.
\end{theorem}

Three ingredients, two of them published:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The ramp (machine-checked).} Adding $R\cdot k$ to any
bounded integer sequence makes it monotone while shifting every
anti-diagonal of its $(\min,+)$-convolution uniformly -- so monotone
MinConv with unbounded values is exactly general MinConv. Sparsified
breakpoint sequences are monotone with unbounded positions; their
merge cannot lean on the fast bounded-monotone MinConv algorithms
(most recently the deterministic $n^{1.5+o(1)}$ of
Jin--Park--Saha--Xu~\cite{JPSX26}).
\item \textbf{The read-off (machine-checked).} Encode a ramped MinConv
instance as two breakpoint lists with unit value-steps: the merged
frontier's inverse at index-sum $m$ \emph{is} the MinConv value at
$m$. Value-only sparsification slack does not blur the read-off: the
positions reported are exact. So a fast merge is a fast general
MinConv.
\item \textbf{The floor (published).} Exact Pareto sum -- the same
task in different clothing -- has no $O(n^{2-\delta})$ algorithm under
the MinConv hypothesis, even when the output has linear
size~\cite{FHSST23}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Why the escape routes close}

\textbf{Rounding positions instead.} Allowing capacity-side error
breaks the merge barrier (that is the ``weak approximation'' world,
where bounded-monotone MinConv applies and
$\OO(n+\varepsilon^{-1.5})$-type bounds exist~\cite{BC22,JPSX26}).
But an FPTAS for \#Knapsack is a \emph{strong} approximation: the
capacity is exact. Any scheme that quantizes positions must, at the
root, resolve the mass within a capacity window around $C$ -- and
isolating that window is NP-hard (its innermost case is counting
exact Subset-Sum solutions, whose nonzeroness is Subset-Sum itself).
Cumulative counting from zero, with exact positions, is forced.

\textbf{Exploiting additive structure of the input.} 3SUM remains
hard on Sidon sets~\cite{JX23}: low additive energy of positions is
not, by itself, an algorithmic lever. Structure helps only when it is
promised (bounded range, monotone values) -- which the ramp shows the
merge does not enjoy.

\textbf{Scope, stated plainly.} The theorem is conditional (MinConv
hypothesis) and framework-level: it binds every algorithm that
computes sum-approximation merges -- which includes all known
deterministic FPTASes for \#Knapsack (Gopalan et al.,
\v{S}tefankovi\v{c} et al., Rizzi--Tomescu, Halman, and~\cite{GMW18})
-- but it is not an unconditional lower bound for the problem. An
improvement now requires either refuting the MinConv hypothesis or a
deterministic framework that avoids position-exact merges entirely
while surviving the window-isolation hardness. Both are
recognized-hard open problems.

\section{Beyond the worst case}

The obstruction is sharply localized: merges are expensive exactly on
additively structured inputs. Empirically (asserted probes in the
repository), a curvature dichotomy holds: windows of the breakpoint
sequence are either near-affine -- compressible by relaxed
piecewise-linear fits, mergeable in closed form -- or curved, in
which case near-minimal ties on anti-diagonals are rare and a pruned
sweep is cheap. An adaptive merge built on this dichotomy is
subquadratic on every structured family we tested, and is blocked
only on the MinConv-hard core, as Theorem~1 says it must be. Making
this a beyond-worst-case theorem -- and porting the resolution-lever
ideas from the randomized side where sampling is permitted -- is the
live continuation.
